# How to Brake Safely on Bali's Hills and Mountain Roads

*Category: Deposit & Safety (cluster) — slug: deposit-safety*

Bali's hills — Kintamani, the roads up to Munduk, the winding descents around Bedugul — are some of the most scenic riding on the island. They also put more strain on your brakes than flat coastal roads ever will, and knowing how to handle a long descent properly makes the difference between an easy ride and a genuinely dangerous one.

## Why Long Descents Are Different

On a long, continuous descent — roughly 100 meters or more of steady downhill braking — your brakes are working far harder than they're designed for in everyday riding.

If the brakes overheat, they're built to protect themselves: they'll temporarily lose effectiveness rather than fail outright, and they return to normal once they've cooled down. It's a safety feature, not a malfunction — but it's much better to avoid triggering it in the first place.

## How to Brake Correctly on a Descent

**Don't ride the same brake continuously without a break.** Constantly holding the front brake alone, the rear brake alone, or both together for an extended descent is exactly what causes overheating.

Instead:

- **Alternate between the front and rear brake** rather than holding one down the whole way.
- **Use engine braking.** Rolling off the throttle lets the engine and variator (CVT) slow the bike naturally, which takes real load off your brake pads.
- **Take short stops on long descents** to let the brakes cool, especially if you're descending for several minutes straight.

One mechanical detail worth knowing: **below about 17 km/h, the variator disengages** and stops providing engine braking. If that happens, a small touch of throttle re-engages it — you'll feel the engine braking kick back in, which helps you control your speed without relying on the brakes alone.

**If you smell anything burning, or the brakes suddenly feel weaker than usual, stop immediately** and give them time to cool before continuing.

## 🏖 Sand and Water on the Road — Pay Attention

If you hit sand or water on the road — especially mid-corner, which is common near beach access roads and after rain:

**Don't use the front brake.** Braking hard on the front wheel on a loose or wet surface is one of the most common ways riders lose front-wheel grip and go down. Instead, switch to gentle, controlled braking with the **rear brake only**, and let the bike settle before you brake harder if you need to.

## The Short Version

Alternate your brakes, lean on engine braking wherever you can, take breaks on long descents, and switch to rear-brake-only the moment you hit sand or water. None of this requires special skill — just a bit of awareness of what the bike is actually doing under you.
