# Deposit Scams: How to Spot Them

*Category: Deposit & Safety (cluster) — slug: deposit-safety*

Most bike rental deposits in Bali are handled honestly — but the scams that do happen get talked about loudly online, and for good reason. Knowing the warning signs before you hand over cash takes away almost all of the risk.

## Red Flag #1: No Photo or Video Record of the Bike's Condition

If a shop hands you the keys without documenting any existing scratches or damage — in photos or video, not just a quick verbal glance-over — you have nothing to point to later if they claim you caused something that was already there. We always photograph the bike ourselves before delivery, and our booking reminder asks clients to do the same:

> 📸 We recommend taking a photo or video of the bike's condition when you receive it and sending it to the chat.

A legitimate company wants that evidence to exist just as much as you do.

## Red Flag #2: Vague or Verbal-Only Deposit Terms

"Just leave your passport" or a deposit amount that changes depending on who you ask is a bad sign. A trustworthy company can tell you the exact deposit amount for your specific bike before you even arrive, and it doesn't move. It's also worth keeping the conversation in chat rather than only spoken out loud — a company with nothing to hide will happily confirm every term in writing.

## Red Flag #3: Your Passport Held as the "Deposit"

This one deserves its own mention: some shops ask for your actual passport instead of a cash deposit. Aside from being risky if you need ID for anything during your trip, it also gives the shop far more leverage than a cash deposit would — walking away from a dispute is much harder without your passport in hand. A cash or bank transfer deposit is the standard, safer practice.

## Red Flag #4: Damage "Found" After the Fact, With No Photos

If a shop claims damage at drop-off that wasn't documented at pickup, and won't show you before/after evidence, that's the classic version of the scam everyone warns about online. This is exactly why photographing the bike yourself at pickup (see our [full deposit guide]) matters — it's your evidence, not just theirs.

## Red Flag #5: No Reviews, No Storefront, No Track Record

A shop that only exists as a WhatsApp number with no reviews, no physical presence, and no way to verify it's a real, ongoing business is the highest-risk setup — not because every small operator is dishonest, but because there's nothing holding them accountable if something goes wrong.

## The Simple Rule

None of this requires detective work. Rent from an established company with real reviews, get the bike's condition documented (ideally with your own photos too) at pickup, and get a clear, fixed deposit amount before you hand anything over. That's genuinely most of the protection you need.
