# How the Deposit Works When Renting a Bike in Bali: Full Guide

*Category: Deposit & Safety (pillar) — slug: deposit-safety*

If you've read any Bali travel forum before booking a scooter, you've probably seen at least one horror story about a rental deposit: shops that "find" damage that wasn't there, deposits that mysteriously never come back, or vague cash amounts nobody explains upfront. It's one of the most common worries first-time renters have — and honestly, a fair one, because a badly-run shop can make it a real problem.

A deposit is standard practice worldwide when renting any kind of vehicle, and Indonesia is no exception. The good news: it's genuinely safe when you rent from an established, reputable company with a real reputation to protect. The actual risk isn't the deposit system itself, it's handing cash to a small, no-name shop that can simply disappear or "find" damage that never existed. Here's exactly how it works with us.

## How Much Is the Deposit

For the vast majority of bikes in our fleet, the security deposit is a flat **1,000,000 IDR**.

There's an exception for premium, higher-value bikes: if you're renting a **Kawasaki ZX25R or Morbidelli** for less than 7 days, the deposit is **2,000,000 IDR**. These are higher-value performance bikes, so short-term rentals carry a higher deposit — the same logic applies at car and bike rental counters worldwide for premium vehicles.

For context: our bikes are worth around 48,000,000 IDR, and the standard deposit is 1,000,000 IDR — roughly 2% of that. Whatever happens, you're never remotely on the hook for anything close to the bike's full value; the deposit exists to cover small, ordinary things, not to be a financial trap.

## First Rental vs. Returning Customers

On your first booking with us, we do take the deposit — it's a fair, standard safeguard for both sides while we're still getting to know each other.

If you've rented from us before and everything went smoothly, we don't ask for a deposit on your next booking. Trust and long-term relationships matter more to us than holding onto a deposit for its own sake.

## Is the Deposit Refundable?

Yes — this is the part people misunderstand most. A deposit is **not a fee**. It's a refundable hold, separate from what you actually pay for the rental itself. Think of it the same way a hotel holds a card for incidentals: if nothing goes wrong, you get the full amount back.

When you return the bike in the same condition you received it, in on time, the deposit is returned to you in full. It only gets reduced if something was damaged, lost, or missing.

It can also work in your favor if your plans change: if you extend your rental, the deposit can usually be applied toward the extension instead of being returned and re-collected — just let us know. For rentals that run past 4 months, this happens automatically.

## What Can Reduce Your Deposit

Damage or loss to rental equipment (not the bike itself) is deducted at set, published rates — not a random number someone makes up on the spot. A few examples:

- A lost helmet or other missing equipment
- A cracked or badly scratched helmet visor
- Lost or damaged original vehicle documents (STNK)
- A lost or broken bike key

Each item has its own fixed rate, so the deduction is predictable and transparent, not negotiable on the spot.

Damage to the **bike itself** works differently — it's assessed individually rather than at a flat rate, simply because bike damage ranges enormously (a small scratch and a cracked fairing are not the same repair cost). This is standard practice at any legitimate rental business, not something unique to us.

## How to Avoid Any Deposit Disputes

A few habits protect you regardless of which company you rent from:

- **Photograph the bike at pickup** — all sides, the dashboard/odometer, and any existing scratches, with timestamps.
- **Ask your rental company to confirm the bike's condition with you** before you ride off, not after.
- **Store the helmet and raincoat in the bike's under-seat compartment or an additional SHAD box** if your bike has one — it's the safest way to keep them secure while parked.
- **Report any incident immediately** rather than waiting until drop-off — this is the single biggest thing that prevents disputes, and it also means we can repair or repaint the affected part before your rental ends, or order a replacement part in advance if needed. Paintwork usually takes about a week, so if you forget to let us know in advance, a fee may apply to cover the bike's downtime, charged at your bike's rental rate.

## FAQ

**Do I need a credit card to pay the deposit?**
Deposits in Bali are typically handled in cash or through the rental company's usual payment method — check with us directly when booking and we'll confirm what works for your dates.

**When exactly do I get my deposit back?**
As soon as the bike is checked and confirmed to be in the same condition it was handed over in — normally right there at drop-off.

**What happens if the bike is stolen while I'm renting it?**
If you have insurance on your rental, this is covered under those terms rather than a flat deposit deduction — the deposit alone is far too small relative to a bike's actual value to cover a theft. Message us directly and we'll walk you through exactly how it's handled for your situation.

**Is the deposit the same for a 2-day rental and a 2-month rental?**
Yes, the deposit amount is tied to the bike model, not the rental length (with the one exception noted above for short-term ZX25R/Morbidelli rentals).
