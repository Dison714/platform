# Bali Traffic Fines for Riders: What They Cost and How to Pay Them

*Category: Legal Guide — slug: legal*

If you've read [Stopped at a Police Checkpoint in Bali: What to Expect], you know a document check is usually routine. This is the follow-up for the less common case — what an actual fine looks like, roughly what it costs, and how it's meant to be paid.

*This is one piece of a bigger picture — for the full overview of the rules that actually apply to riders in Bali, see our main guide: [Bike Rental Laws in Bali 2026: What You Need to Know].*

## Typical Fine Ranges

Exact amounts vary by violation and aren't published as a single official price list, so treat these as a guide rather than a guarantee:

- **No helmet, or helmet not fastened:** the figure most often quoted is around IDR 250,000, though in practice the range is wider — roughly IDR 100,000 to 1,500,000.
- **Missing or invalid IDP / license for the vehicle category:** roughly IDR 250,000 to 1,000,000. Under Indonesia's traffic law (Article 281 of Law 22/2009), driving without a valid license can carry a fine of up to IDR 1,000,000 or, in more serious cases, up to four months of detention. In practice, tourists overwhelmingly see the administrative fine, not detention.
- **Not carrying your STNK (photo):** technically also a documentation violation, but in practice we haven't seen this fined.

There's no single authoritative source listing a fixed amount for every scenario. If an officer quotes you a specific figure and starts writing the ticket, that's the real amount for your situation — the ranges above simply tell you what to expect going in.

## How Fines Are Actually Paid

The official process for a formal ticket (*tilang*) is payment through a bank — commonly via BRI ATMs, tellers, or internet banking using a BRIVA reference number printed on your ticket — not cash handed directly to the officer at the roadside. After payment, you typically bring proof of payment to the police station listed on the ticket to retrieve any documents that were held.

## The Real Downside Isn't the Fine — It's the Impound Lot

For certain violations, your bike can be taken to an impound lot until a court date. The hearing itself is an ordinary procedure with nothing frightening about it. What catches people out is the timing: hearings are often set 1–3 weeks out, and by then the lost rental days plus the cost of getting your bike back from the impound lot add up to considerably more than the fine itself.

The practical takeaway: follow the road rules, and above all ride with a helmet and proper clothing. Those are the most common reasons for being pulled over and fined in the first place — and only then might it come out that you don't have your license, or don't have it on you, which is when the bike can get taken. It's rare, but it happens. And the helmet matters for far more than avoiding a fine — it matters for your own safety.

## Where This Overlaps With Informal "Settlements"

*As we cover in [Stopped at a Police Checkpoint in Bali: What to Expect], a documented pattern exists where a small number of officers offer to accept a smaller cash amount on the spot instead of issuing a formal ticket.* If you'd rather have the official paper trail — which is also the version that actually resolves the matter cleanly — asking for the formal ticket is a reasonable, low-friction way to handle it.

## The Fine Is the Smaller Risk

Worth keeping in perspective: even the higher end of these fines is a fraction of what a single night in a Bali private hospital can cost if you're in an accident without a helmet, without valid documents, and/or with your insurance declining to pay out. *We go through that specific risk in [Riding in Bali Without a License: The Real Risks].* The fine is an inconvenience. The insurance gap is the thing actually worth avoiding.

## The Bottom Line

Fines for missing documents generally fall somewhere between IDR 250,000 and 1,000,000 depending on the violation, paid through official bank channels rather than cash on the spot. The impound lot costs more than the fine ever will — and both are avoidable with a valid license, a matching IDP, a helmet, and sensible clothing.
