# Stopped at a Police Checkpoint in Bali: What to Expect

*Category: Legal Guide — slug: legal*

Getting waved over at a checkpoint (locally called a *razia*) can feel alarming the first time it happens, especially if you've read stories online. In practice, if your documents are in order, it's routine and quick. Here's exactly what to expect and how to handle it calmly.

*This is one piece of a bigger picture — for the full overview of the rules that actually apply to riders in Bali, see our main guide: [Bike Rental Laws in Bali 2026: What You Need to Know].*

## Where Checkpoints Tend to Show Up

Checkpoints cluster around the busiest tourist corridors — main junctions in and out of Canggu, Seminyak, and Uluwatu, and along the routes connecting them. They're usually visible well in advance: a cluster of officers and parked motorbikes on the roadside. Slow down, don't panic, and pull over when signaled.

## What the Officer Will Ask For

The first thing they'll ask for is your International Driving Permit. They may also want your passport (or a copy), your home driving license, and the bike's registration document, the STNK.

A note on the STNK: a photo of it is almost always enough. The original is rarely handed out for rentals, and for good reason — if it's damaged or lost, which is entirely possible on a holiday, replacing it is a slow and expensive process. For your own peace of mind, carry a photo rather than the original.

Keep everything together and easy to reach; fumbling through bags at a checkpoint just makes the stop longer.

*If you haven't sorted your IDP yet, this is the one to read first: [How to Get an International Driving Permit for Bali].*

## If Your Documents Are in Order

This is the most common outcome by far: a quick look, maybe a question or two, and you're waved on. Officers are checking for exactly the things covered above — nothing more is usually needed. Often enough, simply reaching confidently for your documents is enough on its own, and you're sent on your way before anyone actually inspects them.

## If Something's Missing

If a document is missing or your IDP doesn't cover motorcycles, you can be issued a formal ticket. The correct process is that official fines get paid through proper channels — a bank transfer or the state's digital payment system — not handed over as cash on the spot.

## A Word on Informal "Settlements"

It's a documented pattern, reported by outlets including the Jakarta Post, that a small number of individual officers have asked tourists to pay a smaller amount in cash on the spot rather than issuing a formal ticket. This isn't how the official process works, and you're entitled to ask for the formal route instead — a simple, polite request along the lines of "I'd like the official ticket" is usually enough to steer things back to the proper process. It's worth knowing this exists so you're not caught off guard, without assuming it will happen to you — most stops are entirely routine.

## The Bottom Line

Stay calm, keep your documents together and easy to hand over, and treat it like the routine paperwork check it usually is. With a valid license, matching IDP, and a photo of your STNK on hand, there's genuinely nothing to worry about.
