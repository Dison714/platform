Ubud is the simplest route format on Bali: rice terraces, waterfalls just five minutes apart, and temples that don't require winding mountain roads or steep descents in between. It's the best choice for a first route on a regular scooter — not a reason to put off the trip until you rent something more powerful.

## What the route is like

Unlike the eastern or northern directions, there's almost no single "correct" order of stops around Ubud — guests have put together different combinations from the same pool of places, all within half an hour of each other. Here's one such combination as a single day:

> "Hidden Canyon Beji Guwang — a narrow canyon with emerald-green water. Monkey Forest, Ubud — a sacred forest where hundreds of monkeys live right among ancient temples. Alas Harum Bali — swings over the Tegalalang rice terraces. Sebatu Holy Water Spring — a sacred spring deep in the jungle, where Balinese people come, not tourists." — D, June 2026

A calmer, "morning" version of the same area — without rushing between stops:

> "We start out from Ubud. Ride one of the island's most beautiful roads. Have breakfast with a view of the majestic Batur. Around midday — a purification ceremony at one of the island's most powerful sites, the Sebatu springs. Lunch at a local restaurant with a jungle view." — Evgeny Dzen

## Stop by stop

**[Hidden Canyon Beji Guwang](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Yk5MwiR5f8A8yQJb9?g_st=ic).** A narrow canyon with emerald-green water — as described by a guest who went there with a group. The trail inside the canyon is on foot.

**[Monkey Forest](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dLYAPeyHacwiv7ac9?g_st=ic).** A sacred forest in the center of Ubud, with monkeys right among the temples — a classic stop, but that's exactly why it's easy to add to any route without a detour.

**[Alas Harum Bali](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Fm6WpyGqUfnYH93v5?g_st=ic).** Swings over the Tegalalang rice terraces — a paid venue with attractions, not the terrace trail itself.

**An important caveat about Tegalalang.** If you're expecting to simply walk through the famous rice terraces without paid swings and photo spots, it's worth knowing in advance — as one guest who's been through the area more than once describes it:

> "There isn't really one big trail there. It's just a ravine with terraces and attractions, cut up crosswise by fences into individual plots, where each plot owner charges for entry." — Ilia, June 2026

That doesn't mean it's not worth going — just don't expect a free walk through the terraces: the place is set up as a series of separate paid zones, and it's worth building that into your expectations ahead of time.

**[Sebatu Holy Water Spring](https://maps.app.goo.gl/aGCSeSbiftPJnJ2w8?g_st=ic).** A sacred spring used for ritual purification — unlike the tourist bathing pools, this one, according to a guest, is visited mainly by Balinese people themselves, not tourists. It's appropriate to behave accordingly: this is an active place of worship, not an attraction.

**[Kanto Lampo](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qmk8WaGnTADuuQkR8) and [Tukad Cepung](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Cj15HEefbwgdhvd47).** Two waterfalls in the Gianyar area that are most often added to a route heading north, but they work just as well as a separate stop within an Ubud day — both show up in different versions of guest-authored routes as compact "stops along the way."

**[Junglegold Chocolate Factory](https://maps.app.goo.gl/gWt7VYqxkxS54ufr6).** A stop that keeps showing up across several independent guest-authored routes — judging by how often it's mentioned, a reliable choice for a short break.

**[Tanah Lot](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mSMuDVnTBqqMDp6m6) and [Pig Stone Beach](https://maps.app.goo.gl/xJdzaYgp8f568HRy5).** If you're extending the day westward, the ocean-cliff temple of Tanah Lot and the black-sand Pig Stone Beach get added in one version of the route that starts from Seseh, before entering Ubud itself.

## Rafting: Ubud or Sidemen

If you want to add water as well as biking to your day, travelers directly compare the two options:

> "In Ubud it's more romantic and beautiful." — on Ubud rafting, compared with the more extreme alternative

> "We had a great rafting trip in Sidemen yesterday — usually we go to Ubud, but I'd recommend it." — Anne, on Sidemen rafting as a less obvious but worthwhile alternative

Both options require booking a separate tour and aren't part of a self-guided bike route — check with the rafting operator directly.

## Hazardous stretches

Guests don't describe any genuinely hazardous mountain stretches on the Ubud day, unlike the northern and eastern routes — that's one reason the area works well for a first trip. The walking trails inside canyons and to waterfalls can be slippery, especially after rain.

## Which bike to take

Ubud in a day is an ideal route for an automatic scooter: [Honda PCX 160](/bikes?category=honda_pcx160) or [Yamaha Nmax 155](/bikes?category=yamaha_nmax155) — both are easy to park at every stop, and the road between them is flat, with no long climbs. If you're planning to stretch the day into the evening or add stops toward Sebatu/Kintamani, the [Yamaha Xmax 250](/bikes?category=yamaha_xmax250) gives you extra power while staying just as simple an automatic.

A manual bike or anything from the tour-enduro lineup isn't needed here — this is exactly the kind of route where bike power isn't the main constraint on your day.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a good first route after landing in Bali?**
Yes — it's one of the simplest formats: flat roads, short distances between stops, no mountain switchbacks.

**Is it worth going to Tegalalang if I don't want the paid swings?**
You can, but set your expectations first: according to guests, it's not an open walk through the terraces, but a complex of paid zones divided by plot fences.

**Do I need experience riding in Indonesia for this route?**
Less than for the mountain routes — but the basic traffic rules and licensing requirements still apply. We cover the risks of riding without a license in a separate guide: [Riding in Bali Without a License: The Real Risks](/en/blog/riding-in-bali-without-a-license-the-real-risks).

**Can I extend the route toward Kintamani?**
Yes — one version of the Ubud day starts with breakfast overlooking Batur and continues naturally into the mountains — details in [Kintamani: Sunrise at Mount Batur — Route, Departure Time, and What to Wear](/en/blog/kintamani-sunrise-route).
