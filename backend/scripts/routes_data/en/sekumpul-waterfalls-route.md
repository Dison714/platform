[Sekumpul](https://maps.app.goo.gl/T9pKEDoU91tbCbFMA) is Bali's biggest waterfall, and you can actually ride a bike almost all the way there — not walk the whole way, as many people assume: the road to the official ticket booth and parking lot is paved, but the final descent down to the water itself is on foot only, for everyone, regardless of what you're riding. The route's real difficulty isn't the road itself — it's one specific narrow stretch right before the parking lot, which our guests describe outright as unsuitable for beginners.

## What this route is like

Sekumpul comes up again and again in traveler reports as a full-day or weekend destination — here's how participants from at least four different trips described it in the first half of 2026:

> "Weekend getaway in cabins near Sekumpul. Friday 12:00 check-in at the Bali Hidden Space cabins. Saturday — Waroeng Juwet, lunch overlooking the rice terraces. Sekumpul, Hidden, and Fiji waterfalls. Floating down the river. Bonfires, shisha, and dinner in the evening." — trip organizer, January 2026

> "February 7 route 🔥 SEKUMPUL. Meet at 9:00 in Ubud (Canggu and Bukit — 8:30 start). First stop is a golf club for coffee, then off to pick strawberries. After the strawberries, we ride to Bali's biggest waterfall — Sekumpul. We swim there, take photos and video, then head back to Ubud for dinner." — trip organizer, February 2026

A more experienced version of the same route approaches from the north, as part of a two-day Bukit → north motorbike trip: meeting point at [The Community](https://maps.app.goo.gl/TrXAaA7aA9P6bCSc9) → coffee at [Famo Cafe Kayu Putih](https://maps.app.goo.gl/6bR59Waw2tAbKbsq5) → optionally the [Ulun Danu Bratan](https://maps.app.goo.gl/h35DwaLjnTMBqum1A) temple and the [Twin Lake View](https://maps.app.goo.gl/SAkfUoigZxgd1MBz8) viewpoint (Lakes Buyan and Tamblingan) → Sekumpul → overnight nearby → on day two, the lava fields and rice terraces near Kintamani.

## Section by section: paved to the ticket booth, on foot to the water

**The road to the official ticket booth.** Paved, with no surprises for a rider of any experience level.

**The narrow descent before the parking lot.** This is where the real difficulty starts, and it's worth knowing about in advance rather than finding out on the spot — quotes from drivers and travelers who've already been there:

> "You can't drive a car to the waterfall — you can't even reach the warung by car, it's on foot from there." — discussion participant, January 2026

> "Agreed, I've been there twice, I wouldn't recommend it for beginners." — Katerina Kapustina

> "The road isn't great, and closer to the waterfall, on that 'nose-down' stretch, a girl who only got on a bike yesterday really shouldn't be riding it. You definitely can't ride the descent to the waterfall itself — as a beginner, I'd have wiped out for sure, 150%." — discussion participant

It's important to understand exactly what this warning covers: it's one specific narrow, steep stretch of road right before the parking lot, not the entire route. From the official parking lot onward, everyone — beginners and experienced riders alike — walks the rest of the way to the water the same way; the bike isn't part of it anymore.

## Entrance tickets — the thing almost nobody writes about honestly

Here we have something rare for Bali — confirmed prices from two independent sources, months apart:

> "Entry to all three waterfalls is 150k (200k with tea)." — trip participant, January 2026

> "Only buy your ticket at the official booth (a proper building with a parking lot in front of it) — 150,000 for Sekumpul, 250,000 for all three waterfalls. Get all three, because Fiji is beautiful too." — Ilia, June 2026, with an explicit warning about the "mafia" — aggressive freelance guides who offer, for an extra fee, to walk you past the official ticket booth

The bottom line on money as of mid-2026: **150,000 IDR** for Sekumpul alone, **250,000 IDR** for Sekumpul + Hidden + Fiji (all three waterfalls on one ticket). Buy strictly at the official booth — the proper building with a parking lot in front of it — not from people who intercept you on the way in.

## What else to see besides Sekumpul

In one guest's final ranking, after visiting several northern waterfalls in a single trip: "Sekumpul — 100 points, Git Git, Banyumala — a pleasant surprise, Aling-Aling — 50/50." Aling-Aling is part of the same ticket complex as Sekumpul and Hidden ("Fiji") — you can also jump into the water from 5, 10, and 15 meters up there, if that's your kind of thing.

On the way from the south, before Sekumpul, riders often stop at the less-hyped waterfalls in the Sidemen area — [Kanto Lampo](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qmk8WaGnTADuuQkR8) and [Tukad Cepung](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Cj15HEefbwgdhvd47) (both show up in one of our earlier route write-ups as stops on the way to the main northern destination), as well as the [Sidemen Swing](https://maps.app.goo.gl/nz2JDSRh6y3NEx3Y9).

## Where to stay and eat

**[Bali Hidden Space](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qT7o54emessW2VqA7)** — cabins right next to Sekumpul, an option for anyone who wants to stay the night instead of heading back the same day.

**[Waroeng Juwet](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YGSPFAoKvRDWhb5XA)** — lunch with a rice-terrace view, a standard stop on the weekend route to Sekumpul.

If you'd rather stay with a local host, there's [at least one homestay](https://maps.app.goo.gl/WBiCuxRci41ZoNKCA) at the waterfall with a regular owner named Sugi — our guests have contacted him afterward about things they left behind, so it's genuinely possible to follow up after your trip if you forget something.

## Which bike to take

Any bike can reach the official ticket booth and parking lot, including a regular scooter — the asphalt is good. The one place worth being cautious about is the narrow "nose-down" descent right before the parking lot itself: if you don't have much experience, you'll be more comfortable on a bike with a more confident stance and low-rev torque, such as the [Honda ADV 160](/bikes?category=honda_adv160) or [Suzuki V-Strom 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=suzuki_vstrom250), rather than a light city scooter. If you're riding with a group that includes a beginner, one guest's approach was sensible — leave the bike a little before the tricky stretch and walk it, especially since the whole way down to the water is on foot regardless.

For the longer version of the route — Sekumpul plus the continuation toward Kintamani and the lava fields — the [Yamaha Xmax 250](/bikes?category=yamaha_xmax250) or [Kawasaki Versys-X 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=kawasaki_versys) work well: more range and a more comfortable riding position for two days back to back.

## Seasonality

We haven't yet collected specific driver input on how the rainy season changes this particular narrow descent before the parking lot (mud, slippery rocks on the way down to the water). Given that the stretch is already flagged as difficult for beginners in dry weather, it's worth building in extra caution during the rainy season.

## FAQ

**Can you ride a bike all the way to the waterfall?**
No — from the official parking lot to the water, everyone walks; it doesn't depend on riding experience or bike type.

**Should you get a ticket for all three waterfalls, or is Sekumpul alone enough?**
According to a guest who visited all of them — "Fiji is beautiful too" — and the price difference is small (150k versus 250k for all three). If your schedule allows it, get the combined ticket.

**Should a beginner rider skip this route entirely?**
Not the whole route — just the final narrow descent before the parking lot. If that stretch feels beyond your riding experience, it's reasonable to leave the bike a little earlier and walk it; the rest of the way is on foot anyway.

**Can you combine Sekumpul with a trip to Kintamani?**
Yes, that's a common two-day route format — details in our guide [Kintamani: Sunrise at Mount Batur — Route, What Time to Leave, and What to Wear](/en/blog/kintamani-sunrise-route).
