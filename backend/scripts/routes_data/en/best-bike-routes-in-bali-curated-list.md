There's no single "best route" in Bali — just different directions suited to different bikes and different amounts of time. East Bali (Amed, Tirta Gangga, the temples) suits travelers who want one long day or two unhurried days. The central waterfalls and Kintamani are for early risers and mountain roads. A one-day Ubud loop is the simplest format on a scooter. Below, each direction gets its own article with real waypoints, quotes from our drivers, and the bike that's actually worth taking.

## Routes by Region

- **[East Bali by Bike: From Ubud and Bukit to Amed via Lahangan Sweet and Tirta Gangga](/en/blog/east-bali-amed-route)** — temples, waterfalls, and coastline, 1–3 days, best on an adventure-touring bike.
- **[Sekumpul and the Waterfalls of North-Central Bali: Route, Prices, and Where You'll Have to Walk](/en/blog/sekumpul-waterfalls-route)** — Bali's biggest waterfall and a descent not everyone makes it down.
- **[Kintamani: Sunrise at Mount Batur — Route, What Time to Leave, and What to Wear](/en/blog/kintamani-sunrise-route)** — a regular early-morning route with hot springs and lava fields.
- **[Ubud in One Day on a Scooter: Rice Terraces, Waterfalls, and Temples Without Mountains or Switchbacks](/en/blog/ubud-route-rice-terraces-and-temples)** — the simplest format, well suited to a scooter.
- **[Bedugul – Munduk – Lovina: A Mountain Route to North Bali in 2 Days](/en/blog/bedugul-munduk-lovina-route)** — 2 days, lakes, waterfalls, dolphins at sunrise.
- **[Bukit Peninsula by Bike: Uluwatu, Jimbaran, Nusa Dua, and Sunset Spots Without Heading North](/en/blog/bukit-peninsula-route)** — short rides that stay entirely in the south of the island.
- **[Bali's Volcanoes by Bike: Getting to the Trailheads for Agung, Abang, and Batukaru](/en/blog/bali-volcano-treks-route)** — where riders actually park before the climb.

## How These Articles Work

Every point in these articles comes from a real traveler or guest, with a date and, where it makes sense, a direct quote. Where we don't have exact data — entrance ticket prices, precise peak-hour travel times, or the condition of a specific stretch of road — we don't fill in approximate numbers. That's a deliberate choice: a made-up detail a traveler won't find on the ground damages trust more than an honest "check locally." We update these gaps as fresh input comes in from drivers and guests.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I ride these routes on a regular scooter?**
Most of the stops, yes. In every article we specifically flag where the road gets harder — a steep switchback, a gravel stretch, a descent people walk down — and when it's worth taking something more powerful than a scooter.

**How current are the prices and travel times in these articles?**
Prices and details mentioned by our drivers and guests themselves are given as-is, with the date of the source. Every six months we recheck road conditions, entrance ticket prices, and travel times — check the article's last-updated date for the latest review.
