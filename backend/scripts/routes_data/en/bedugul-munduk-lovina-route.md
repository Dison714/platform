The route through Bedugul and Munduk to [Lovina](https://maps.app.goo.gl/xwFNXP3MsZUcB1wS6) takes in [Lake Beratan](https://maps.app.goo.gl/h35DwaLjnTMBqum1A), the Jatiluwih rice terraces, [Banyumala](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YHt1PguKTgQuLM829?g_st=ic) and Gitgit waterfalls, and finishes with a night swim among bioluminescent plankton and a morning dolphin encounter. The road to Lake Beratan is easy, evenings in Munduk are genuinely cold, and the walk down to some of the waterfalls is physically harder than it looks in photos — your knees will remember it.

## What this route is like

Based on travelers' own accounts, this direction has been ridden at least three different ways — from a relaxed two-day version with an overnight in Lovina to a one-day, 150-km push with no loops:

> "Left Ubud around 10:00 in the morning. First stop, Lake Beratan. Second stop, Sekumpul waterfall, maybe Banyumala twin falls too. Arrived in Lovina in the evening, stayed the night, then went out for the glowing plankton, and swam with dolphins in the morning." — Alex F1, two-day route

> "Heading north: Canggu → Tanah Lot → Jatiluwih → the mountain lake → waterfalls → Lovina. Day one, 150 km, 20 people. All one direction, no loops." — D, May 2026

A more classic version through the island's center, with a full list of stops in a single message:

> "Tomorrow we're heading to Lovina through Bali's best spots: Jatiluwih Rice Terraces — huge UNESCO-listed rice terraces. Ulun Danu Beratan Temple — a temple on the water up in the mountains. Handara Gate — the famous Balinese gate everyone photographs. Gitgit Waterfall — one of the most beautiful waterfalls in the north of the island. Lovina Beach — a quiet stretch of coast in the north with black volcanic sand. Next day, out on the ocean at sunrise to watch dolphins." — Aizhan, March 2026

Looking back on the trip, one traveler summed it up simply: "The most beautiful part of this trip was the road itself!"

## Point by point

**Tanah Lot.** A temple on a rock in the middle of the ocean — the starting point for routes that come in from the west, via Canggu.

**Jatiluwih.** UNESCO-listed rice terraces — a stop that shows up again and again in almost every version of this route.

**Lake Beratan and Ulun Danu Beratan Temple.** A temple on the water high in the mountains — one of Bali's most recognizable sights. According to one guest's independent account, the road here is used as the benchmark for an "easy" road when comparing it to tougher routes: "as easy as the road to Beratan."

**Handara Gate.** The classic Balinese photo gate — a quick, compact stop along the way.

**Gitgit and Banyumala waterfalls.** Gitgit is one of the best-known waterfalls in the north of the island. Banyumala (a twin waterfall) earned special praise from a guest who visited several waterfalls on the same trip: "especially surprised me." Both are close to each other in the Bedugul area.

**Munduk.** A separate cluster of stops came up in response to a traveler's question about "what's a must-see in Munduk" — [Bali Farm House](https://maps.app.goo.gl/D3LXGQ7PhRiNLAkK9), [Secret Lodge](https://maps.app.goo.gl/LSQghUwVQjCjeCvg8), [Bali Strawberry Farm & Resto](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JGfxZt6x9bW9pYT77) with its panoramic terrace, and the [botanical garden](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dMzyeEySE9ViEDuF8). [Munduk Escape](https://maps.app.goo.gl/SnDH9wiiizckwBLD9) café / Munduk Sari Resort gets praised for its sunset view at a low price — but with an important warning about the weather:

> "It's cool there all the time. And in the evening it's freezing." — a guest on Munduk evenings

Bring warm clothes for the evening, even if it was hot down on the coast during the day.

**Nungnung Waterfall.** Another waterfall in the region that gets singled out for the road itself: "The road there is a joy... one of my favorite waterfalls, probably only beaten by Sekumpul for scale." But the walk down to the water is physically harder than it looks in photos:

> "You need to be careful on the way down too, otherwise your knees will be stacked in a pile by the bottom." — another guest on the same waterfall

**Lovina.** The finish line for most versions of this route — black volcanic sand and a paired activity: a night swim among bioluminescent plankton and a morning boat trip out to see dolphins. It's worth confirming the schedule in advance: "Dolphins are the next morning, since apparently they're best seen early too" — based on one guest's experience, the dolphin boats head out early, almost right after sunrise.

**A hidden option with flying foxes and a Buddha statue.** One guest described a side road with light traffic and a small stretch of switchbacks as a find of its own: "Turn right there and you're in for a treat — light traffic, great views, even a bit of a switchback, and the road surface is decent. Stop and check out the flying fox and the biggest Buddha statue in Bali." The exact turn wasn't named in the message, so treat this as a lead worth chasing down, not a ready-made instruction.

## Dangerous sections and traffic

Beyond the physically demanding walk down to Nungnung, one guest warns directly about local drivers' habits in the mountainous northern part of the island:

> "The scenery up there is unbelievably beautiful... but the traffic and the way locals drive is honestly on another level." — a guest on the northern mountain roads

For a breakdown of safe braking technique on long descents, see the dedicated guide: [How to Brake Safely on Bali's Hills and Mountain Roads](/en/blog/how-to-brake-safely-on-balis-hills-and-mountain-roads).

## Which bike to take

The road to Lake Beratan and Jatiluwih is easy — it's the same stretch people use as the benchmark for an "easy" road — so a [Yamaha Xmax 250](/bikes?category=yamaha_xmax250) is plenty for that part of the route. But this is a two-day, 150+ km route with cold evenings in Munduk and uneven traffic in the mountains closer to the north — for the full version, a touring enduro is more comfortable: [Suzuki V-Strom 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=suzuki_vstrom250) or [Keeway Road Falcon 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=keeway_roadfalcon250) — an upright riding position and a windscreen matter on a long, cold ride through the mountains. [Kawasaki Versys-X 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=kawasaki_versys) is also a solid choice for this direction.

## FAQ

**Can you do this route in one day?**
Per one traveler's report — yes, around 150 km one way with no loops, but it's a packed day with no overnight stop. Two days with a night in Lovina is more comfortable.

**Is a regular scooter okay for this?**
As far as Jatiluwih and Lake Beratan — yes. For the full route with cold evenings in Munduk and the more demanding mountain section up north, a touring enduro is more comfortable.

**Is it better to swim with dolphins in Lovina in the evening or the morning?**
Dolphin trips go out in the morning, early, almost right after sunrise. Swimming with the bioluminescent plankton is a separate evening activity on the day you arrive.

**Can this route be combined with Sekumpul?**
Yes — one version of the route includes a stop at Sekumpul and Banyumala along the way. More details here: [Sekumpul and the Waterfalls of Central North Bali: Route, Prices, and Where You'll Have to Walk](/en/blog/sekumpul-waterfalls-route).
