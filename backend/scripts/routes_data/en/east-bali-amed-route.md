The East Bali route is Lahangan Sweet with sunrise views of Mount Agung, the Tirta Gangga water palace, the Lempuyang temple complex, and Amed's black volcanic sand coastline; as a one-way trip from Ubud or Bukit it's a full day, and with an overnight stay in Amed it's an unhurried two to three days. The road is mostly paved and comfortable on any bike, but as you get close to Amed itself it narrows and winds — that's where it pays to be more careful, or to ride something with more power than a scooter.

*This article is part of our overall guide to Bali's motorbike routes: [Bali Motorbike Routes: A Guide from MDB Motor Rental's Drivers and Clients](/en/blog/best-bike-routes-in-bali-curated-list).*

## Route Character

Our guests ride this route in different ways — some fit the main stops into one long day, others stretch it into two or three days with a night in Amed. Here's how one of our drivers described the route after guiding a group along the east coast:

> "We head out to Amed at 9:00 from Denpasar. We'll stop at some great spots along the way, do some snorkeling, swing by a water palace, check out a lighthouse, and catch the sunset. The next day we'll go to a beautiful viewpoint and visit the temple where everyone takes that Instagram shot at the gate with Agung in the background. Then more snorkeling, this time in Amed." — group trip participant, March 2026

A more spread-out, three-day version of the same route (from a participant in a different group) adds a few more stops and a practical order to visit them in:

> "Monkey Bar at Bella Vista — the best spot in the morning: clouds, Agung, jungle, fewer people, about 1 hour. Then [Gembleng Waterfall](https://maps.app.goo.gl/wF9ggv8wkjomBmaU9) — natural pools, jungle atmosphere, about 1 hour. Jagasatru Waterfall — fewer people, a good stop between Sidemen and the east, about 40 minutes. BERINA Rice Terrace — a small, scenic stop, 20 minutes max, and after that comes the best stretch of road of the whole trip on the way to Amed." — group participant, May 2026 (the same route was repeated almost word for word by another group in June 2026)

## Stage by Stage: What's Along the Way

**Start of the route.** Groups set off either from Ubud/Bukit or from the [Pertamina](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Z3uLFWPAhi8op6Fh8) gas station on the way east — one driver described a typical morning start like this: "07:00 — start with breakfast in Canggu, they've got the best coffee in the area. First stop is the Pertamina gas station."

**Monkey Bar / Sidemen — waterfalls.** The first part of the route runs through the Sidemen area: Gembleng Waterfall (natural pools, jungle setting) and the lesser-known Jagasatru Waterfall — according to one guest, it draws fewer crowds than the popular waterfalls and makes a convenient stop roughly halfway to the east.

**BERINA Rice Terrace → Amed.** A short stop at the rice terraces, then — according to a guest who rode this stretch twice with two different groups — "the best road section of the whole trip" on the way to Amed.

**[Lahangan Sweet](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3i6UHehwoubwQo827).** A viewpoint with a tree and a gate framed against Mount Agung — the most frequently repeated stop across every version of the east route. A tip from a guest who came here early:

> "Get here by around 8 a.m. — there's a chance you'll see the sailing fishing boats coming back in." — group participant, comment on the Lahangan Sweet stop

Another participant called it "the strongest sunrise spot of the whole trip" — it's worth building the sunrise here into your plan as a separate, early stop if your schedule allows it.

**[Tirta Gangga](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Adr7ZYwRSYvkEHzu5) and [Taman Ujung](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mFVrQWQXV9ze8vmbA).** Two water palaces in east Bali — Tirta Gangga ("built in a Versailles-inspired style," as one guest described it) and Taman Ujung, a former royal residence — both are worth walking slowly, taking your time between the ponds and pavilions.

**[Pura Lempuyang](https://maps.app.goo.gl/iXbpvHH5WoV8wkYC6) ("Gates of Heaven").** If you're adding the famous temple complex with views of Agung to your route — advice from a guest who visited with a group:

> "We're not going to stand in line here for the photo at the gate, we'll just walk around the complex itself." — group participant, January 2026

The line for the classic "in the gate" photo can take an hour or more during peak season; if that specific photo isn't a must, you can explore the whole complex at your own pace without waiting in line at all.

**Amed.** A coastline of black volcanic sand, and a gateway to diving and snorkeling. The best spot for snorkeling from the shore, according to one guest, is [Jemeluk](https://maps.app.goo.gl/j8FDvwtnTAafK9Te7) bay: "This is the most comfortable beach in Amed for snorkeling. Nice sand, sun loungers, beautiful coral. Sometimes you'll see turtles in the morning." Nearby is Lipah Beach (a sandier option) and the USAT Liberty shipwreck dive site in Tulamben.

**[Virgin Beach](https://maps.app.goo.gl/rKteTKBfyy3rzFX39) (Bias Tugel).** A frequent final stop on the east route — white sand and turquoise water tucked between green hills, as one guest described it. Most groups follow the same formula: arrive → coconut → swim (the sea here is calmer than on the more exposed beaches) → grab some fish to eat → rest before the ride back. There's camping on site if you're planning to stay the night.

## Tricky Sections and What Our Drivers Say

The approach to Amed itself is the most demanding part of the route. One of our drivers described the road toward the port like this:

> "There's nowhere to speed, the road toward the port is dangerous — bikes can cross your path there, then there are switchbacks and not much light... switchbacks and the road isn't great." — traveler's comment, June 2026

## Gas, Parking and Food

The Pertamina gas station on the way out of the south of the island is the standard starting point for groups heading east; roadside Pertamini stalls (bottled gasoline) can be found along the whole route, but we haven't personally checked how far apart they are on this particular direction yet.

The restaurant "[Rimba](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3uSSgy3Az2T9prZn7)" in Amed is popular enough that guests sometimes scout a backup option in advance in case there's no table — which is itself a decent pointer if you're looking for somewhere to eat in Amed.

Parking at most of the main stops — Lahangan Sweet, Tirta Gangga, Virgin Beach — is organized, but we don't yet have exact rates from guests for this route.

## Which Bike to Take on the East Route

If your whole plan is to reach Lahangan Sweet, the palaces, and come back the same day on the main road, a [Yamaha Xmax 250](/bikes?category=yamaha_xmax250) or any maxi-scooter handles it without any trouble — the road for this part of the route is mostly smooth. Where the scooter's comfort runs out is exactly where the good asphalt runs out — on the approach to Amed itself, and especially on the road toward the port, which the drivers themselves describe as "switchbacks and not great" — that's where it's nicer to be on a bike with a more upright riding position and some extra power in reserve for the long climbs.

For the two-to-three-day version of the route with a night in Amed, adventure-touring bikes are the best fit: the [Suzuki V-Strom 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=suzuki_vstrom250) (6-speed gearbox, 12-liter tank) or the [Kawasaki Versys-X 250](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=kawasaki_versys).

If you're riding as a group and someone prefers a manual gearbox over an automatic, but with a more planted feel on rough sections, the [Honda CB150X](/bikes?group=motorcycle&model=honda_cb150x) or the adventure scooter [Honda ADV 160](/bikes?category=honda_adv160) also work well for this route.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you really do the whole east route in one day?**
Yes, if you stick to the main stops (Lahangan Sweet, one of the palaces, Amed) and set off early — most of the groups who shared their experience started between 7:00 and 9:00. If you also want Lempuyang and proper downtime in Amed, it's more comfortable spread over two days.

**Do you need to book snorkeling in advance?**
Based on guest feedback, the dive centers in Amed take walk-ins without a reservation, but check the specific prices and conditions on-site — we don't have up-to-date pricing directly from the dive centers.

**Is a regular scooter okay for a one-day trip?**
Yes, for the one-day version up to Lahangan Sweet and the palaces, it's perfectly fine. It gets more demanding specifically on the approach to Amed's port, as covered in detail above.

**Can you add Nusa Penida to this route?**
No, that's a separate destination — the island is only reachable by ferry, and you'd either have to leave the bike on the mainland or ship it across separately, with document checks (STNK and license) at boarding. We'll cover that in a separate guide.
